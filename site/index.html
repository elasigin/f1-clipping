Formula 1, 2025 and 2026 qualifying telemetry

Flat out and slowing down

In 2026, Formula 1 cars sometimes lose speed on a straight while the driver still has the throttle wide open. This article finds those moments in lap data and shows where they happen.

What is clipping?

Below is the same driver, Kimi Antonelli, on the same straight at Spa in two seasons. Antonelli is at full throttle the whole way in both. In 2025 the car keeps gaining speed, reaching 341 km/h. In 2026 it gets to 328 km/h, then goes slower and slower until the brakes.

The likely reason is the battery. A 2026 car's electric motor may deliver up to 350 kW, up from 120 kW before (F1.com). But the charge may only swing by 4 MJ (FIA Technical Regulations, C5.2.7 and C5.2.9). At full power that lasts about 11 seconds. Antonelli's 2026 lap of Spa has 67 seconds at full throttle. Braking and part throttle put energy back, within a limit per lap, but the car still has to ration it.

When the help runs short, the car is clipping. In super clipping the motor goes further and works as a generator: it takes power from the engine to charge the battery, at full throttle. The driver's foot never moves.

Public telemetry has no battery channel, so nothing here measures clipping directly. I look for its footprint: full throttle, no brake, speed falling. That fits both kinds of clipping and cannot tell them apart. The 2025 cars are the control. If the method found clipping there, it would be too loose.

First, 2026 is quicker

Out of La Source the 2026 car pulls ahead, as a stronger motor would suggest. At 800 m it is doing 317 km/h against 297.

Slowing that doesn't count

Up Eau Rouge, both cars lose speed at full throttle. They are cornering hard, uphill. Fast corners slow a car whatever the battery does, so I leave out any stretch cornering above 1.5 g.

Kemmel: the help runs out

The straight from the top of the page. At 1,600 m the 2026 car is still ahead, 328 to 322 km/h. At 2,000 m it trails by 18. Two zones here (hatched) fit clipping, and the second loses 20 km/h.

Blanchimont: a lower bound

From 336 km/h the 2026 car falls to 286, while 2025 holds near 316. But most of it happens in a fast corner, so only 105 m counts. The real total is probably higher. The gap in the orange line is missing telemetry.

Added up

In 2026, 700 m of 5,090 m at full throttle (13.8%) fits clipping. In 2025, none does. How sure is that? Stricter rules find 0.0% and looser ones 18.8%, because the slowing is gentle. Trust the pattern more than the number.

Across the season

Now the same test on one qualifying lap per team, at every circuit both seasons share. Clipping shows up on 128 of 198 laps in 2026, and on 1 of 180 in 2025.

The track matters far more than the team. At Miami the median team clips on 18.1% of its full-throttle distance. At 6 of 18 circuits the median is zero. Team averages sit between 6.6% and 8.7%.

Long flat-out runs go with clipping. All 7 circuits with a run over 1,200 m show it. But length isn't everything: Mexico City has a run of 1,020 m and a median of zero. The FIA can also set a lower charging limit at some circuits, which this data can't separate out.

I can't say who improved. Rules changed from Miami, every circuit differs, and nearly every team's gap to the circuit median is small enough to be chance. Compare teams within a column, not along a row.

Method and limits

What counts. A zone is marked where the throttle is at least %, the brake is off, speed is above km/h, and the car loses at least km/h over m or more. Stretches cornering above g are left out. The headline number is the clipped share of full-throttle distance.

  • Inferred, not measured. There is no battery data. Wind is not corrected for, and neither are slopes: Kemmel climbs about 4.5%, but the 2025 car gains speed on the same climb.
  • A lower bound. Clipping inside fast corners is dropped, and a car that merely accelerates less is not counted.
  • The motor also fades by rule. In qualifying, overtake mode is available for the whole lap (Sporting Regulations, B7.2.3). It allows the full 350 kW up to 337.5 km/h, fading to zero at 355, unless the FIA adjusts it for a circuit (Technical Regulations, C5.2.7 and C5.2.8). 188 of 223 zones found in 2026 start below 337.5, so this rule alone doesn't explain them.
  • The cars differ in other ways. 2026 has active aerodynamics and no DRS, so the speed gap is not all battery. Lap time differences are not a cost of clipping.
  • One lap per team. Drivers choose where to spend energy, and one lap is a small sample. Exact percentages depend on the thresholds, so the season chart shows the range from stricter to looser rules.
  • Telemetry is coarse and sometimes broken. Real samples arrive about four times a second. Frozen or missing 2026 data is left blank, never shown as zero, and some laps were replaced by the team's next best.
  • What is missing. Las Vegas 2025 and Interlagos 2026 were wet, so they have no dry pair. The 2026 rounds at Kuala Lumpur and Madrid have no 2025 race to compare with. Qatar and Abu Dhabi 2026 had not been held when this was built.

The rules moved. On 20 April 2026 the FIA announced that peak superclip power would rise from 250 to 350 kW from Miami, with a lower qualifying charge limit meant to cut super clipping to roughly 2 to 4 seconds a lap (FIA). The August Technical Regulations let the FIA lower the limit at individual events (C5.2.10). The slowing found here lasts a median 2.4 seconds a lap from Miami on, but it is not the same quantity.
